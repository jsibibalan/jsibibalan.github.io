Thanks for stopping by!  I am Sibi (short for Sibibalan Jeevanandam), a PhD candidate in the School of Mechanical Engineering at Purdue University. I am advised by Prof. Neera Jain.

My graduate research lies at the intersection of hybrid dynamical systems, state estimation, set-based methods, and human-machine interaction. The goal of my dissertation is to enable conditionally automated vehicles (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-driving_car#automation-levels) to be aware of the human driver they interact with. A human-aware vehicle could use estimates of its user's cognitive states (e.g., trust and workload) to adjust its behavior (e.g., amount of information displayed to the user) to improve safety. 

Working towards this goal, I've used a hybrid dynamical model to describe the evolution of the user's cognitive states; a hybrid dynamical model can handle mixed (continuous-valued and discrete) variable types. I treat the cognitive states as continuous-valued, while a human user's reliance on automation is discrete (they use it or they don't). I've then characterized the process and measurement uncertainties as bounded and not probabilistic; I believe independent identically distributed random variables may not describe the un-measurable factors that influence human behavior well.  

Outside of graduate school, you can find me being an amateur at road cycling, climbing, running, gymming, or playing tennis.
